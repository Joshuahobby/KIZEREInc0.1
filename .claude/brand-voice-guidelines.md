# KIZERE Brand Voice Guidelines

**Version 1.0 — June 2026**
**Owner: KIZERE Inc. — Brand & Product**

## 1. Brand Identity

### One-Sentence Truth

KIZERE is building the infrastructure for trusted global ownership — giving every physical item a verified digital identity so it can be registered, verified, protected, recovered, and transferred with absolute certainty.

### Tagline

**The Identity of Things**

This phrase is never fragmented, paraphrased, or riffed upon. It appears in full or not at all. It is the strategic register anchor — infrastructure-level, categorical, permanent.

### Promise

**Own It. Prove It. Protect It.**

Three declarative imperatives. No softening. Used in pitch materials, press, and hero surfaces. Not a tagline — a commitment statement.

### Method

Register, verify, and protect what you own — instantly.

### Strategic Analogy

"The TCP/IP of physical ownership." Use in investor and press contexts only. Not for consumer-facing copy.

### The Three-Layer Model

KIZERE operates at three layers simultaneously. Every communication lives in at least one layer. Know which layer you are writing for.

**Layer 1 — Lost and Found Platform**
The consumer entry point. Where Distressed Owners and Good Samaritans interact. Voice is warm, direct, resolution-oriented. Consumer register applies.

**Layer 2 — Item Registry and Theft Prevention**
The ownership layer. Where items get registered, verified, and protected. Voice bridges both registers: personal benefit meets systemic reliability.

**Layer 3 — Ownership Infrastructure**
The platform layer. Where valuation, API integrations, and institutional partnerships live. Voice is fully strategic. Infrastructure language. No consumer emotional framing.

### What KIZERE Is Not

These distinctions are not optional — they define how KIZERE is positioned in every external communication.

- Not a lost-and-found app (Layer 1 is the entry point, not the whole product)
- Not a blockchain project (no blockchain is involved; do not invite that association)
- Not a government database (KIZERE is independent infrastructure)
- Not a startup idea (KIZERE is operational infrastructure — present tense, declarative)

## 2. Voice Architecture: The Two-Register System

KIZERE speaks in two distinct registers. Both are intentional. Both are correct. The error is using the wrong register in the wrong moment.

### Strategic Register

**Lead phrase:** The Identity of Things
**Frame:** Infrastructure, protocol, systems scale
**Emotional register:** Gravity, confidence, permanence
**Applies to:** Homepage hero, pitch decks, investor materials, press releases, API documentation, B2B landing pages, Layer 3 communications

**Characteristics:**
- Speaks at systems scale, not feature scale
- Short declarative sentences with institutional weight
- No consumer emotional framing
- Infrastructure vocabulary: registry, protocol, primitives, chain of title, immutable, tamper-proof
- Refers to the problem as a structural gap, not a personal inconvenience

**Example:**
> "Physical ownership has no digital truth layer. KIZERE is building it — permanently. Every item. Every transfer. Every verification. Absolute certainty."

**B2B example:**
> "KIZERE provides protocol primitives that telecoms, insurers, e-commerce platforms, law enforcement, and financial institutions can build on top of."

**What the strategic register never does:**
- Use "peace of mind" or emotional reassurance language
- Speak to individual loss scenarios
- Use "you" in the intimate second person
- Hedge: no "we aim to," "we hope to," "we are trying to"

---

### Consumer Register

**Lead phrase:** Secure What Matters Most
**Frame:** Personal ownership, recovery, protection of things that carry meaning
**Emotional register:** Warmth, clarity, resolution
**Applies to:** Onboarding flows, transactional emails, feature descriptions inside the app, notification copy, lost/found report flows, Help Center, any moment a user is mid-task

**Characteristics:**
- First name always in emails: "Hello [Name]!"
- Resolution always present alongside risk — urgency without alarm
- Benefit-led: what happens for the user, not what KIZERE does technically
- Community framing: "A community network dedicated to helping you find what's lost"
- Plain, unadorned sentences; no layered adjectives

**Example:**
> "Your Samsung Galaxy S22 is now protected. If it's ever lost or stolen, anyone who finds it can scan the QR label to help return it to you."

**Email example:**
> "Hello Francine! Good news — someone has found an item matching your report. We've notified them of your claim."

**What the consumer register never does:**
- Use infrastructure vocabulary (protocol, primitives, chain of title) without explanation
- Speak about KIZERE's strategic mission
- Describe the product as a platform or registry in its first sentence
- Use alarm language — "stolen," "danger," "threat" — without immediate resolution framing

---

### Register Decision Rule

Ask: Is this person in the middle of a task, or evaluating KIZERE from the outside?

- **Mid-task, mid-flow, mid-emotion:** Consumer register.
- **Outside, evaluating, deciding, investing, integrating:** Strategic register.

When in doubt, consumer register is safer. A Good Samaritan who just found a lost item does not need to know KIZERE is the TCP/IP of physical ownership.

## 3. Writing Principles

### 3.1 Declare, Never Hedge

KIZERE copy states facts, not intentions. The product is built. The infrastructure exists. The capability is real. Write in present tense with active voice.

- ❌ "We aim to give every item a verified digital identity."
- ✅ "Every item gets a verified digital identity."
- ❌ "We hope to make recovery easier."
- ✅ "Recovery starts the moment you register."

### 3.2 Mechanism First, Human Value Second

In feature descriptions and technical surfaces, explain how something works before explaining why it matters. This builds credibility before making the emotional claim.

- ❌ "Feel safe knowing your items are protected with our advanced registry system."
- ✅ "Your item is assigned a tamper-proof digital identity. If it's ever lost, anyone who finds it can verify ownership instantly."

### 3.3 Sparse Headlines

No five-word adjective chains. No compound superlatives. Headlines earn weight through brevity and directness.

- ❌ "Comprehensive Intelligent Digital Item Protection Platform"
- ✅ "The Identity of Things"
- ❌ "Advanced Multi-Layer Security for All Your Valuables"
- ✅ "Instantly dangerous for thieves to sell."

### 3.4 Urgency Without Alarm

Loss and theft are named plainly. They are not euphemized. But resolution is always present. Never end on threat; always pivot to capability.

- ❌ "Your valuable items could be stolen or lost at any time."
- ✅ "Items get lost. Theft happens. KIZERE is built to fix this — permanently."

### 3.5 Community Without Sentimentality

KIZERE exists because people help each other. That is stated as fact, not as inspiration. The Good Samaritan is not a hero — they are a participant in a functioning system.

- ❌ "Our incredible community of kind-hearted heroes helps reunite lost items with their owners."
- ✅ "A community network dedicated to helping you find what's lost."

### 3.6 Empathy in Transactional Moments

When a user has lost something, they are distressed. Emails and notification copy in these moments must be warm, personal, and get to the resolution immediately. No brand philosophy. No product positioning. First name. Clear action.

- ❌ "KIZERE has detected a potential match for your lost item report using our advanced matching system."
- ✅ "Hello James! Good news — we found a potential match for your lost laptop. Review it now."

### 3.7 Technically Credible, Not Technically Overwhelming

KIZERE can speak to engineers, insurers, and law enforcement without losing a rural market vendor in Huye. The mechanism is explained simply. Jargon is earned, not assumed.

- Strategic context: "Tamper-proof, immutable records on every registered item."
- Consumer context: "Your ownership record can never be altered or deleted."

### 3.8 American English Throughout

"Organization" not "organisation." "Recognize" not "recognise." "Program" not "programme." This applies to all copy including developer documentation, legal text, and translated-language source strings.

## 5. Terminology Reference

### Preferred Terms

| Term | Definition and Usage |
|------|----------------------|
| **Register** | The action of adding an item to KIZERE. "Register your laptop." Never "list," "upload," or "add." |
| **Verify / Verification** | Confirming ownership, identity, or status. "Confirm" is acceptable in UI micro-copy only (e.g., confirmation dialogs). |
| **Protect** | Primary CTA verb. "Protect your valuables." Never "save." "Secure" is secondary only. |
| **Recover** | What happens when a lost item is returned. Never "retrieve" or "get back." |
| **The Good Samaritan** | The person who found an item and reported it. Not "finder" or "reporter." Title-cased when referring to the persona. |
| **The Distressed Owner** | The person who lost an item. Not "victim" or "user." Title-cased when referring to the persona. |
| **Ownership Certificate** | The formal proof-of-ownership document. Not "receipt," "title," or "deed." |
| **Digital Passport** | Informal term for the digital identity record of an item. Acceptable in consumer copy. Not "digital copy" or "online record." |
| **Chain of title** | The formal record of ownership transfers. "Chain of custody" acceptable in law enforcement contexts. |
| **Tamper-proof** | The property of the ownership ledger that prevents alteration. Not "secure" for this specific meaning. |
| **Immutable** | Stronger than "permanent" — use in technical and press contexts. |
| **Infrastructure** | KIZERE's strategic self-description at Layer 3. Reserve for first-impression positioning. |
| **The Identity of Things** | The tagline. Full phrase, title-cased. Never fragmented or paraphrased. |
| **KZR-XXXXXX** | Human-readable display label on certificates, receipts, dashboards. Monospace, uppercase, wide letter-spacing. Never stored in DB; never in public URLs. |
| **POS-XXXXXX** | Display prefix for POS-registered products. Same typographic treatment as KZR-XXXXXX. |
| **Organization** | American English. Never "organisation." |

### Prohibited Terms

| Avoid | Reason |
|-------|--------|
| List / upload / add (for items) | Implies a directory; weakens the registry framing |
| Victim | Disempowering; The Distressed Owner is an agent, not a passive subject |
| Finder / reporter | Generic; loses the moral framing of the Good Samaritan |
| Receipt / title / deed | Too casual or too jurisdiction-specific |
| Blockchain | KIZERE does not use blockchain; the association is misleading |
| Startup idea | KIZERE is operational; this language undermines credibility |
| Lost-and-found app | Reduces Layer 3 infrastructure to Layer 1 entry point |
| Hero (for the Good Samaritan) | Sentimental; KIZERE's community is functional, not inspirational |
| We aim to / we hope to / we try to | All hedging language is prohibited |
| Dodger Blue #3EBDE8 | Deprecated visual identity; do not use |
| Cardinal Red #C41B2B | Deprecated visual identity; do not use |
| Organisation | Wrong spelling for this brand's American English standard |

## 6. Tone by Context

### Hero / First Impression

**Register:** Strategic | **Formality:** High | **Energy:** Declarative, impactful

Maximum confidence. Short sentences. Imperative or declarative. No qualifications, no adjective chains, no questions that undermine certainty.

> "Physical ownership has no digital truth layer. KIZERE is building it."

---

### Feature Descriptions

**Register:** Consumer or bridge (Layer 2) | **Formality:** Medium | **Energy:** Clear, direct

One clear sentence per feature. Name the mechanism, then name the benefit. Avoid superlatives.

> "QR Tags — Generate a secure label for any item. When someone finds it, they scan the tag to help return it to you."

---

### Transactional Emails

**Register:** Consumer | **Formality:** Warm and personal | **Energy:** Calm, resolved, direct

First name always. Get to the point in sentence one. No brand philosophy. Resolution language whenever there is a problem.

> "Hello Robert! Your claim for the Samsung Galaxy S22 (KZR-004821) has been approved. The item is ready for pickup."

---

### Error and System Messages

**Register:** Neutral | **Formality:** Functional | **Energy:** Flat, no personality

State what happened. State what the user can do. No exclamation points. No personality injected into failure states.

> "Verification failed. The information provided did not match our records. Please review your details and try again."

---

### CTA Buttons

**Register:** Consumer (most surfaces) / Strategic (pitch/landing) | **Energy:** Benefit-forward, imperative

| Context | ✅ Correct | ❌ Incorrect |
|---------|-----------|-------------|
| Primary registration CTA | Protect Item | Submit |
| Account creation | Get Started — Free | Sign Up |
| Verification | Verify Item | Check |
| Recovery flow | Report Lost Item | Continue |
| B2B demo | Request Access | Contact Us |
| Claim submission | Submit Claim | OK |

---

### B2B / Business-Facing

**Register:** Strategic | **Formality:** High | **Energy:** Measured, metric-anchored

More formal sentence structure. Reference measurable outcomes. Infrastructure vocabulary throughout. No consumer emotional framing.

> "KIZERE's registry API gives insurers real-time ownership verification at the point of claim — reducing fraud exposure and accelerating settlement cycles."

---

### Press and Investor

**Register:** Strategic | **Formality:** High | **Energy:** Authoritative

Company described in present tense as operational infrastructure. Rwanda and East Africa rootedness is an asset, not a caveat.

> "KIZERE is building the TCP/IP of physical ownership — a verified digital identity layer for every item in the physical world, beginning in East Africa."

## 7. Audiences

### The Distressed Owner

Someone who has lost or had stolen a valuable item. In a state of distress or urgency at the moment of interaction.

**Voice notes:** Consumer register throughout. Warmth before information. Resolution language immediately visible. Never alarm language without resolution. Short sentences, clear next steps.

> "Hello Francine. Your lost item report for your Samsung Galaxy S22 is now active. If anyone in our network finds it, you'll be notified immediately."

---

### The Good Samaritan

Someone who found an item and wants to do the right thing. May not know KIZERE — the QR scan may be their first contact.

**Voice notes:** Consumer register. Warm, appreciative, never transactional. Simple, clear instructions — assume no prior KIZERE knowledge. Acknowledge the good act without sentimentality. No registration pressure at first contact.

> "You've found a registered item. The owner has been notified. Provide your contact details so we can facilitate the return."

---

### The System Guardian (Admin)

A KIZERE internal administrator with full platform access.

**Voice notes:** Functional language — labels, statuses, actions. No consumer emotional framing. Infrastructure vocabulary appropriate. Error messages must be precise and actionable.

> "User verification pending. OTP issued 4 minutes ago. No response. Escalate or resend."

---

### The Official Verifier (Moderator)

A trusted intermediary reviewing contested ownership claims with institutional authority.

**Voice notes:** Formal and procedural. Chain of title language is appropriate. Present evidence without editorial comment. Actions must be clearly labeled.

> "Review the proof of ownership provided below. Chain of title documentation is attached. Confirm or reject this claim."

---

### The Business / Subscriber

Retailers, insurers, NGOs, government agencies, or logistics operators using POS, batch tools, or API integrations.

**Voice notes:** Strategic register. B2B tone rules apply: metric-anchored, ROI-adjacent. Reference business outcomes. Infrastructure vocabulary throughout.

> "Your POS terminal is connected. Batch registration is active for 847 items. Ownership certificates are being issued automatically."

## 8. Messaging Pillars

Six core messages underpin all KIZERE communications. Know which pillar your content serves before writing.

### Pillar 1: Ownership as Digital Truth

**Core idea:** Physical ownership currently has no verified digital layer. KIZERE is building it.

**Primary phrase:** "Every physical item a verified digital identity."
**Supporting phrase:** "Physical ownership has no digital truth layer — until now."
**Where it leads:** Hero copy, investor materials, press, B2B pitch, Layer 3 communications

---

### Pillar 2: Recovery and Reunion

**Core idea:** Lost items can be recovered. The community network makes return possible.

**Primary phrase:** "A community network dedicated to helping you find what's lost."
**Supporting phrase:** "Your item is never truly gone — it's registered."
**Where it leads:** Consumer onboarding, lost report flows, email communications, testimonials

---

### Pillar 3: Theft Deterrence and Protection

**Core idea:** Registration makes theft economically irrational. Registered items cannot be laundered through resale.

**Primary phrase:** "Instantly dangerous for thieves to attempt to pawn or sell it."
**Supporting phrase:** "Registration is the strongest deterrent available."
**Where it leads:** Feature descriptions, registration flow, item protection emails, retailer/insurer pitches

---

### Pillar 4: Infrastructure and Platform

**Core idea:** KIZERE is not just a consumer app — it is a protocol layer that other institutions build on.

**Primary phrase:** "Protocol primitives — the building blocks that telecoms, insurers, e-commerce platforms, law enforcement, governments, and financial institutions can build on top of."
**Supporting phrase:** "The TCP/IP of physical ownership."
**Where it leads:** Investor materials, API documentation, B2B landing pages, press, Layer 3 communications

---

### Pillar 5: Trust and Verification

**Core idea:** Every claim, every transfer, every ownership record is verifiable and tamper-proof.

**Primary phrase:** "Secure. Verifiable. Transferable."
**Supporting phrase:** "Built on Trust and Transparency."
**Where it leads:** Claim flows, Ownership Certificate copy, verification screens, B2B trust signals

---

### Pillar 6: Rwanda and East Africa Rootedness

**Core idea:** KIZERE works at every level of economic access, from a Nokia feature phone in a rural market to a tech-forward urban consumer in Kigali. Built here, scalable globally.

**Primary phrase:** "Works on a Nokia feature phone in a rural market as easily as it works for a tech-forward urban consumer in Kigali."
**Supporting phrase:** "Built in Rwanda. Built for the world."
**Where it leads:** About page, investor materials, press, B2B market context, i18n-relevant copy

## 9. Visual Identity

### Color Palette

| Role | Hex / Class | Usage |
|------|-------------|-------|
| Primary background | `#0B0F1A` | All primary surfaces |
| Primary accent | `#2563EB` (blue-600) | Buttons, primary interactive elements |
| Secondary accent | `#22d3ee` (cyan-400) | Highlights, hover states, border on interaction |
| Tertiary | Indigo-400/500 | Supporting UI, secondary labels |
| Premium highlight | Yellow-300/400 | Sparingly — "Go Premium" CTA only |
| Email header gradient | `linear-gradient(135deg, #1e3a8a 0%, #3b82f6 100%)` | Email headers exclusively |

**Deprecated — do not use under any circumstances:**

| Value | Status |
|-------|--------|
| Dodger Blue `#3EBDE8` | Fully superseded — April 2024 spec |
| Cardinal Red `#C41B2B` | Fully superseded — April 2024 spec |

---

### Typography

**Primary typeface: Geist**
Geometric, technical, open source. Used for all brand typography including marketing, product, and documentation.

**Fallback: Inter**
Only when Geist is unavailable. Never a design-first choice.

**Weight and treatment:**

| Context | Weight | Tracking |
|---------|--------|----------|
| Headlines | font-black (900) | tracking-tight |
| Uppercase labels | medium | tracking-widest |
| Body text | regular or medium | default |
| Identifiers (KZR-XXXXXX, POS-XXXXXX) | monospace | tracking-widest |

The typeface conveys technical credibility and geometric clarity. Do not substitute display or serif typefaces for brand-facing surfaces.

---

### Card and UI Style

- **Surface:** Dark glass morphism — dark background at 40% opacity + backdrop-blur
- **Border at rest:** `border-white/10`
- **Border on hover:** `border-cyan-400/40`

This system must be consistent across all surfaces. Do not introduce drop-shadows, bright fills, or light-background cards in KIZERE-branded interfaces.

---

### Logo

- **URL:** https://kizere.rw/icons/icon-512x512.png
- Do not distort, recolor, or apply effects to the logo
- Minimum clear space: equivalent to the cap height of the "K" in KIZERE on all sides

---

### Item Identifiers

**KZR-XXXXXX** — generated from auto-increment DB ID as a human-readable display label. Appears on Ownership Certificates, receipts, and dashboards.

**POS-XXXXXX** — display prefix for POS-registered products.

**Typographic treatment for both:** Monospace, uppercase, `tracking-widest`. These are cosmetic display labels only. They are not stored in the database. They must never appear in public-facing URLs.

## 10. Language and i18n

### American English Standard

All source copy uses American English spelling and conventions — including developer documentation, legal text, and UI strings.

| ✅ American English | ❌ British English (not used) |
|--------------------|------------------------------|
| organization | organisation |
| recognize | recognise |
| program | programme |
| color | colour |
| center | centre |

This rule applies to all content including `KIZERE-TRUTH.md`, `AGENTS.md`, `CLAUDE.md`, and any governance documentation.

---

### Four Supported Languages

KIZERE's platform supports four languages with locale files in `client/src/lib/i18n/locales/`:

1. **English (en)** — Source language. American English. All new copy is written here first.
2. **French (fr)** — Full platform support. Formal register (vouvoiement) in consumer surfaces.
3. **Kinyarwanda (rw)** — Full platform support. Cultural-voice adaptation required (see below).
4. **Swahili (sw)** — Full platform support. Cultural-voice adaptation required (see below).

---

### Two-Register System in All Languages

The strategic register and consumer register architecture applies in all four languages. Translators must understand both registers before beginning work. A translation that renders the strategic register in consumer-register language — or vice versa — is incorrect regardless of linguistic accuracy.

---

### Cultural-Voice Adaptation Notes

**Kinyarwanda (rw):**
- The Good Samaritan concept maps well to existing community values — lean into mutual aid framing
- Ownership language must be sensitive to communal vs. individual property norms in Rwandan context
- "Digital Passport" informal term may need a culturally resonant equivalent
- Infrastructure register should convey institutional trust, not government authority

**Swahili (sw):**
- East Africa regional framing (Pillar 6) should reference Swahili-speaking markets explicitly where appropriate
- Consumer register warmth should reflect interpersonal directness common in Swahili communication styles
- Verify that "The Identity of Things" renders with equivalent conceptual weight in Swahili, not as a feature description

---

### i18n Workflow

1. Write new copy in American English (`en.json`) first
2. Add the key to all four locale files simultaneously — `en.json`, `fr.json`, `rw.json`, `sw.json`
3. Use the `t()` hook: `t('key', 'fallback string')`
4. Run `node audit-locales.cjs` from project root to detect missing keys before committing
5. Never hardcode visible strings in component files

## 11. Governance

### Document Ownership

This document is owned by KIZERE Inc. — Brand and Product. Current maintainer: founding team. As the organization grows, a dedicated Brand Lead will assume ownership.

**Document path:** `.claude/brand-voice-guidelines.md`

---

### What Requires a Guidelines Update

- Any change to the tagline, promise, or one-sentence truth
- Addition of a new messaging pillar or audience segment
- Change to the color palette or typography stack
- Deprecation or addition of a preferred/prohibited term
- Expansion to a new language

---

### What Does Not Require a Guidelines Update

- New copy written consistently with existing guidelines
- New feature copy that applies existing pillar and register rules correctly
- Locale file additions that follow established register architecture

---

### How to Use This Document

**Engineers:** When writing UI string keys, error messages, notification copy, or CTA labels — check Section 5 (Terminology) and Section 6 (Tone by Context) first. The preferred terms table is your primary reference.

**Designers:** Visual Identity is in Section 9. Deprecated color values are explicitly listed — do not use them. Card style and typography treatment are canonical. When a component deviates from these rules, the component is wrong, not the guidelines.

**Copywriters and Marketers:** Read Sections 2 (Register), 3 (Principles), 4 (Do/Don't), and 6 (Tone by Context) in full before writing any external communication. Know which audience (Section 7) and which pillar (Section 8) you are serving before beginning a draft.
